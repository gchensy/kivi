Conventions#

Every public function of kivi follows the rules on this page. The module pages and the guides take them for granted, and the docstrings mention only where a function departs from them or adds to them.

Imports#

kivi has no top-level imports. Import each function from its module, as in from kivi.woe import woe_table; each module has its own page in this reference.

Only the names that a module lists in __all__ are public. Compatibility says which of them a release may change, and how it announces the change.

kivi needs only NumPy and pandas. kivi.plotting also needs matplotlib, which the plot extra installs (pip install "kivi[plot]"); it imports matplotlib only when it draws a chart, so importing kivi never requires it.

Arguments#

  • The data come first and the target follows them, as in woe_table(groups, target), roc_auc(score, target), stepwise(X, target) and fit_spec(frame, target). A function with several data arguments takes all of them before the target, as swap_set(current, new, target) does, and a predicted probability comes after the target, as in calibration_table(groups, target, probability). The back-test of continuous estimates follows the same order, with the realized values in the place of the target and the estimates in that of the probability, as in backtest_table(groups, realized, estimated), and generalized_auc() takes the estimated segments as its data, like a score, and the realized ones as its target, as in generalized_auc(estimated, realized). scikit-learn’s metrics take the target first, so check the order when porting code from them.

  • Data that describe other observations, such as the rejected applicants, follow the data and the target of the main sample, as in parceling(score, target, reject_score) and in reweighting(score, reject_score), which has no target. In the same way, the payments of delinquency_panel() follow its schedule, as in delinquency_panel(loan, due_date, amount_due, payment_loan, payment_date, payment_amount), and the cash flows of workout_lgd() follow its defaulted loans, as in workout_lgd(loan, default_date, ead, flow_loan, flow_date, flow_amount, rate=rate), with positive recoveries and negative costs.

  • The functions of kivi.features take tables with named columns, as fit_spec() and profile_table() do: first the events or the monthly panel, then observations, with one row per entity and observation time, such as the applicants and their application dates, as in window_features(events, observations, id=..., time=..., observation_time=...). The names of the columns are keyword arguments. id names the entity in both tables, and its values are matched by equality, so 1 and "1" are different entities.

  • X is a numeric model matrix, as in stepwise(), logistic_regression(), selection_path(), vif(), vif_filter(), correlation_filter() and fractional_regression(). The response of fractional_regression() is a continuous target from 0 to 1, such as a realized LGD, where a binary one is called target. frame holds raw columns, as in fit_spec(), profile_table(), rule_candidates() and BinningSpec.cut, and woe is the matrix of WOE values that woe_to_points() turns into points.

  • rules is a DataFrame of policy rules in kivi.rules, with one column per rule, named after it, and one row per applicant, as in rule_table(rules, target). A column holds booleans, or 0 and 1, and is True where the rule hits, that is where it declines or flags the applicant. rule_set_table() applies the rules in the order of the columns.

  • matrix is a square matrix of pairwise comparisons in kivi.expert: a DataFrame whose index and columns hold the same factor labels in the same order, or an array or a nested list, whose factors are numbered from 0. Its diagonal must be 1 and its entries reciprocal, \(a_{ij} a_{ji} = 1\), to the relative tolerance of the keyword rtol, 1e-3 by default, which accepts reciprocals rounded to four decimals, such as 0.3333 for 1 / 3. The matrix of transition_pd() is a transition matrix instead, whose rows are probabilities that add up to 1.

  • grades lists the grades of a rating system from the best to the worst, at least two, in migration_bandwidth() and migration_test(). They take the grade of each customer at the beginning of the period and its status at the end, as in migration_test(before, after, grades=grades), like transition_matrix(). Every value of before must be a grade, while a status at the end that is not one, such as "default" or "closed", counts only among the customers of the grade at the beginning.

  • realized and estimated are continuous values, such as the realized LGD of a closed default and its estimate, which may lie outside [0, 1]. For generalized_auc() they are ordered segments: numbers in the order of their values or an ordered categorical in the order of its categories.

  • A predicted probability of an event, such as a PD, is always called probability, as in brier_score(probability, target), calibration_table(groups, target, probability) and expected_loss(probability, lgd, ead). What a score is depends on the module; see Targets, scores and probabilities. A second probability is named after its meaning, as origination_probability in significant_increase().

  • An option with the same meaning has the same name in every module, such as bins, special, smoothing, unseen and weights. level is the confidence level of an interval or a bound, a number strictly between 0 and 1 that defaults to 0.95, as in roc_auc_interval(), generalized_auc() and most_prudent_pd(). The level of capital_requirement() is the confidence level of the one-factor model, 0.999 by default as the IRB approach sets it, and that of conditional_pd() the quantile of the economy, given instead of a factor.

  • bins defaults to 5 for the bins of a variable, as in bin_edges(), fit_spec() and profile_table(), and to 10 for the bands of a score, as in gains_table(), monitoring_table(), parceling() and reweighting(). The cutoffs of cutoff_table() and confusion_table() default to 10 bands as well.

  • asset_class selects the risk-weight function of kivi.capital: "corporate", "hvcre", "mortgage", "revolving" or "other_retail". It is keyword-only and has no default, because the class decides the formula. correlation there is an asset correlation of at least 0 and below 1, and above 0 in systematic_factor(), and turnover and maturity are given in the units of the rules, such as millions of euros and years.

  • event is a count of events per group where the rows are groups, as in fit_grade_pd() and most_prudent_pd(), which take it with total, and as the event column of the tables. In survival_table() it is a 0/1 flag per account instead, next to competing, the flag of an exit for another reason, such as an early repayment; the two are never both 1 on a row. duration is the period in which an account defaulted, left or was last observed, a positive whole number, and entry the number of periods before it came under observation.

  • special is a list of codes for a function of one variable, and a dict by column for fit_spec(), profile_table() and rule_candidates(), which reject a list.

  • Some options have similar names but different meanings. The min_share of category_groups() is the share below which a category counts as rare, while the min_bin_share of chimerge_edges(), tree_edges(), ks_edges(), monotonic_edges(), optimal_edges() and variance_edges() and the min_group_share of chimerge_groups() set the smallest bin or group of the result, and the min_bin_events of optimal_edges() the fewest events, and non-events, of a bin, in units of weight. The min_hit_rate of rule_candidates() is the smallest share of all rows that a rule must hit. Only monotonic_edges() accepts max_bins=None, which merges bins only until the WOE is monotonic. Its direction is "increasing", "decreasing" or "auto", while the trend of optimal_edges() names a shape of the WOE as woe_trend() does, U shapes included, and that of variance_edges() the same shape of the mean response. unseen says what to do with a group that a table lacks and defaults to "raise", while unseen_points is a number of points or None. method names the binning method in bin_edges(), fit_spec() and rule_candidates(), the correlation in correlation_filter() and the estimate of the weights in ahp_weights(). rate is a rate per period in expected_credit_loss() and an annual rate, one for all loans or one per loan, in workout_lgd().

  • The loss and revenue of cutoff_table() are amounts per applicant, finite and non-negative and matched like the target, and the profit of a cut-off is its revenue less its losses, so costs such as funding or acquisition belong in loss. The no_loss_share, full_loss_share and roi of expected_maximum_profit() describe a portfolio instead: the probabilities that a default loses nothing and that it loses the whole loan, and the return of a good loan as a share of the amount lent.

  • A reference that could be read in two ways has one argument for each. The functions of kivi.monitoring take either reference_period, a label of the period argument, or a reference sample in reference_score or reference_groups, and raise unless exactly one is given.

  • The data, and the fitted results that a function applies, such as the edges of cut() or the table of woe_transform(), are positional arguments. Every option after them is keyword-only. The number of bins of bin_edges() is the one option that can also be passed by position:

>>> from kivi.binning import bin_edges
>>> bin_edges([1, 2, 3, 4], 2)
array([-inf,  2.5,  inf])
>>> bin_edges([1, 2, 3, 4], 2, method="uniform")
array([-inf,  2.5,  inf])
>>> bin_edges([1, 2, 3, 4], 2, "uniform")
Traceback (most recent call last):
    ...
TypeError: bin_edges() takes from 1 to 2 positional arguments but 3 were given

Inputs and results#

  • A one-dimensional input is array_like: a list, a NumPy array or a pandas Series. A function that works on several columns at once, such as stepwise() or fit_spec(), takes a DataFrame.

  • The binning functions read their values with pandas.to_numeric, so strings of numbers and booleans count as numbers there. Special codes must not be booleans, because True == 1, and fit_spec() groups a boolean column as categorical instead of binning it.

  • Dates, such as the due dates and payment dates of delinquency_panel(), the default and flow dates of workout_lgd() and the application dates of make_transactions(), are read with pandas.to_datetime, as datetimes or as strings. Numbers raise an error instead of being read as nanoseconds since 1970, the time of day is ignored, and a date with a time zone keeps its local date. workout_lgd() and make_transactions() also reject periods, which .dt.to_timestamp() converts.

  • The time columns of kivi.features are stricter: they must already hold datetimes, and strings raise an error that suggests pandas.to_datetime, because parsing the strings of a large event table is slow and its format can be ambiguous. They keep the time of day. The times of the events and of the observations are both tz-naive or both in one time zone, compared by name, and any other combination raises; with a time zone, the bounds of a window are computed on the local calendar and the events compared as instants. The month of panel_features() may hold monthly periods or datetimes, and months and observation times are each read on their own calendar, so tz-aware months and tz-aware observation times must share one time zone.

  • A result with one value per input value is shaped like the input. A Series gives a Series with the same index and name, other array_like input gives a NumPy array, and a single number gives a float. cut() and assign_grades() return groups, so for input other than a Series they return a pandas.Categorical:

>>> import pandas as pd
>>> from kivi.calibration import prior_correction
>>> rates = dict(sample_rate=0.5, population_rate=0.02)
>>> prior_correction([0.5, 0.2], **rates).round(4)
array([0.02  , 0.0051])
>>> prior_correction(pd.Series([0.5, 0.2], index=["a", "b"], name="pd"), **rates).round(4)
a    0.0200
b    0.0051
Name: pd, dtype: float64
>>> round(prior_correction(0.5, **rates), 4)
0.02

  • A Series result takes the name of the first data argument, as the results of pd_to_score(), cut() and woe_transform() do, so the scores of a Series named pd are named pd too. A result that is a new quantity is named after it: woe() names its result woe, expected_loss() expected_loss, significant_increase() significant_increase, assign_stages() stage, reweighting(), ahp_weights() and combine_weights() weight, asset_correlation() correlation, capital_requirement() capital_requirement, conditional_pd() conditional_pd, systematic_factor() factor, most_prudent_pd() most_prudent_pd and conversion_factor() ccf. The value-by-value functions of kivi.capital broadcast their inputs against each other, so one call covers a portfolio with a PD, an LGD and a maturity per exposure and one asset class.

  • Some inputs must be one-dimensional even for one exposure. significant_increase() and assign_stages() raise for a single number, and expected_credit_loss() takes one row per account, so pass the single curve of convert_pd() as curve[None, :].

  • Series that describe the same observations, such as the data, the target, the probabilities and the weights, must have identical indices; they are not aligned. Any other combination of these inputs is matched by position. Inputs that describe other observations, such as the two samples of psi_table(), the rejected applicants of parceling() or the payments of delinquency_panel(), can have any index. Counts per group, as in woe(), iv() and psi(), describe the same groups, so two Series of counts must have identical indices too.

  • Tables are DataFrames. A table of the groups passed in, such as that of woe_table(), is indexed by the group labels, in an index named bin. Other tables name the index after their rows, such as variable, grade, cutoff, threshold, statistic, step, period, cohort, start, state, stage, loan, rule or before, and name their columns when these are labels too, such as horizon, after or month_on_book. Compatibility lists the index and the columns of every table. A total is the sum of a column, such as woe_table(groups, target)["iv"].sum().

  • The features of kivi.features keep the index of observations, in its order, so that they join back to the observations. Their names follow from the arguments: the column names and the prefix are kept as given, and only kivi’s own parts of the names, the aggregations and the windows, are in lower case, so Amount and amount give Amount_sum_3m and amount_sum_3m.

  • Named statistics come back as a Series or a DataFrame with an index named statistic, as from roc_auc_interval(), generalized_auc(), brier_decomposition(), concentration_test(), migration_bandwidth(), ahp_consistency() and expected_maximum_profit(), or as a Series indexed by the names alone, as from delong_test(). Parameters that another function applies come back as a tuple, such as the slope and the intercept that platt_fit() and quasi_moment_fit() return for platt_predict(), the edges and the probabilities that isotonic_fit() returns for isotonic_predict() and the long-run PD and the correlation that vasicek_fit() returns for conditional_pd(), and so does the statistic and p-value pair of hosmer_lemeshow() and spiegelhalter_test(). A single fitted parameter is a float, such as the shift of the log odds that central_tendency_shift() returns, which the caller adds to the log odds of the PDs or to the intercept of a model.

  • A chart of kivi.plotting takes the table that a kivi function returned first, and the Axes to draw on as the keyword-only ax; without ax it draws on a new figure. It returns the Axes, never shows the figure and leaves matplotlib’s global settings alone. The text it writes is only column names, argument names and values, so one image serves every language.

  • Functions do not modify their inputs. Random draws come from a local generator seeded by rng, so they never change NumPy’s global state, and the same seed gives the same result.

  • rng takes what numpy.random.default_rng takes, such as None, an integer, a SeedSequence or a Generator. A Generator is used and advanced rather than copied, so two calls with the same Generator give different draws, while two calls with the same integer agree.

Targets, scores and probabilities#

  • A target is 1 for an event, such as a default, and 0 for a non-event. Any other label, and a missing label, raises an error. swap_set(), monitoring_table(), rule_candidates(), rule_table() and rule_set_table() are the exceptions: they read a missing label as an outcome that is not known, such as that of a declined applicant or of a loan whose outcome window has not ended, and their target is optional, except in rule_candidates(), which needs the outcomes to search.

  • A response is a continuous outcome where a target is binary, such as the realized LGD or CCF of each defaulted loan, in variance_edges() and fractional_regression(). It holds finite real numbers, which may leave [0, 1] in variance_edges, as LGDs above 1 do, and must lie in [0, 1] in fractional_regression. Missing values, infinities and booleans raise an error.

  • The bad argument of vintage_table() is the status of a loan in a month, such as being 90 days past due, rather than the target of an observation. It takes booleans or 0 and 1, without missing values, and so does the defaulted of default_rate_table(), whether a loan is in default in a month. The bad of panel_features() is the first bad category of an ordered status column instead, "30-59" by default: it and every worse status count as bad. The status column must be an ordered categorical, from the best status to the worst, so that labels such as current are never sorted alphabetically.

  • In kivi.metrics, kivi.monitoring and kivi.strategy, a score rises with the event probability. Pass a PD as it is, and negate scorecard points, which fall as the risk rises. A cut-off of kivi.strategy approves the scores at or below it.

  • In kivi.scorecard, a score is scorecard points, which fall as the risk rises: score_to_pd() gives a lower PD for a higher score.

  • platt_fit() accepts a score in either direction and fits a negative slope to one that falls as the risk rises. isotonic_fit() assumes a rising score unless increasing=False, and quasi_moment_fit() needs a score that rises with the risk, such as the number of a grade counted from the best.

  • True and False count as 1 and 0 in targets, scores and probabilities, and in the aggregations of window_features(), where the sum of a flag counts the events that have it. Weights, LGDs, exposures, losses, revenues, the default_rate of systematic_factor() and vasicek_fit(), the amounts of delinquency_panel(), workout_lgd() and conversion_factor(), the response of variance_edges() and fractional_regression(), the realized and estimated values of backtest_table() and generalized_auc() and the entries of a comparison matrix reject them, and so do most numeric options, such as bins, max_bins, factor and the cut-offs. Six inputs reject them because a boolean there is more likely a mistake: the reference_score of monitoring_table(), which would usually be a row mask, the matrix of transition_pd(), the stage of stage_table(), the days_past_due of assign_stages(), and the duration and entry of survival_table():

>>> import numpy as np
>>> from kivi.metrics import roc_auc
>>> roc_auc([0.1, 0.9, 0.4], [False, True, True])
1.0
>>> from kivi.strategy import expected_loss
>>> expected_loss(np.array([0.1, 0.2]), np.array([True, False]), 100.0)
Traceback (most recent call last):
    ...
ValueError: lgd must be real and numeric, got dtype bool

Groups and missing values#

The functions that count groups, such as woe_table(), psi_table() and calibration_table(), take the labels as they are: the intervals of pandas.cut or kivi.binning.cut(), strings, numbers or categories.

  • Missing values form a group of their own.

  • Only observed groups appear. Unused categories are left out, and so are the groups whose weights sum to zero. Tables with a fixed set of rows are the exception and keep their empty rows: stage_table() always has stages 1, 2 and 3, swap_set() always has its four rows, and the period tables of kivi.monitoring keep a row for every category of a categorical period, so that the rows of all reports line up.

  • Categorical groups, including the intervals of pandas.cut, follow the order of their categories, with the missing group last. Other labels follow the order in which they first appear.

  • Two samples, as in psi_table(), follow the order of the categories only when both have the same categorical dtype. Otherwise, as for an ordered and an unordered categorical, their groups follow the order in which they first appear, in the reference sample and then in the current one.

>>> from kivi.woe import woe_table
>>> woe_table(["b", "a", None, "a"], [1, 0, 1, 0])[["non_event", "event"]]
     non_event  event
bin
b            0      1
a            2      0
NaN          0      1

cut() returns the ordered categorical of pandas.cut when there are no special codes, and an unordered one with special, because the special codes come first and have no order with the intervals.

Categories are matched to a fitted table or grouping by equality, and a category that it does not know raises an error unless unseen says how to treat it. woe_transform(), group_categories() and csi_table() share this rule, the last through unseen_points. reason_codes() matches points rather than categories, and its unseen_points exempts the points that woe_transform gave unseen groups.

The functions that fit edges or groupings, such as bin_edges(), chimerge_edges(), optimal_edges() and category_groups(), leave missing values out of the fit, and cut() and group_categories() keep them missing, so that they form a group of their own when counted. rule_candidates() leaves them out of the fit of its thresholds too, and a missing value hits none of its rules. variance_edges() leaves out the missing values of values with their responses, but raises for a missing response.

The functions that count, rank or fit, such as roc_auc(), calibration_table(), cutoff_table() and platt_fit(), raise an error for a missing score or probability instead, so decide how missing values are scored before passing them in. The functions that convert each value on its own, such as pd_to_score(), platt_predict() and expected_loss(), return NaN for a missing value, and assign_grades() returns a missing grade. The staging functions of kivi.lifetime work value by value but raise for a missing value, because a missing PD or number of days past due must not decide a stage, and the functions of kivi.monitoring raise for a missing period. The value-by-value functions of kivi.capital return NaN for a missing PD, LGD, default rate, factor or level, but raise for a missing turnover, maturity or correlation, which decides the formula. survival_table(), vasicek_fit() and most_prudent_pd() count or fit their inputs as a whole and raise for a missing value. workout_lgd() raises for a missing loan, date, exposure, amount or rate, lgd_table() for a missing LGD and fractional_regression() for a missing driver or response, while conversion_factor() converts each line on its own and returns NaN. delinquency_panel() raises for a missing loan, date or amount, and the functions of kivi.expert for a missing comparison or weight. default_rate_table() raises for a missing loan, month or defaulted flag, gives a missing grade a row of its own, listed last, as the EBA guidelines ask for obligors without a rating, and checks probability only on the rows that start a cohort, so that the rows of defaulted loans may leave it empty. central_tendency_shift() and quasi_moment_fit() fit their inputs as a whole and raise for a missing PD or score. rule_table() and rule_set_table() raise for a missing value in a rule, because whether a rule hits when its data are missing is part of the rule, so decide it with fillna first. The validation tests raise for a missing value as well: backtest_table() for a missing realized or estimated value, generalized_auc() for a missing segment, concentration_test() for a missing count, and migration_bandwidth() and migration_test() for a missing grade or status, so give the customers who left the grades a status of their own. The functions of kivi.features raise for a missing entity, time, month or status, and leave the missing values of an aggregated column out of its aggregations, while the events features count every event.

Sample weights#

Most functions that count observations or fit a model take weights: one finite, non-negative number per observation, matched to the data like the target. psi_table() and csi_table() take one set for each sample, reference_weights and current_weights.

weights belongs to the main data, and the weights of another sample are named after it, as the reference_weights of monitoring_table() and stability_table(). vif(), vif_filter(), correlation_filter(), parceling() and reweighting() take no weights and give every row the same weight, and so do the functions of kivi.features, which count each event once. concentration_test() takes the counts or the exposures of the grades instead of rows, and so do most_prudent_pd(), which takes the obligors and defaults of each grade, and vasicek_fit(), which takes one default rate per period. The value-by-value functions of kivi.capital work on single exposures and take no weights. The validation sample of selection_path() counts each row once.

  • Counts become sums of weights, and observations with zero weight are left out. Integer weights therefore give the same counts as repeating each row that many times.

  • Weighted quantiles use the inverted CDF, so every interior quantile edge is an observed value, while unweighted quantiles interpolate between observations. Weighted edges therefore agree with repeated rows only where no interpolation is needed, and weights of 1 do not give the same edges as no weights.

  • smoothing replaces an empty cell in units of weight and is not rescaled with the weights.

  • logistic_regression(), stepwise(), selection_path(), roc_auc_interval(), delong_test(), generalized_auc(), spiegelhalter_test(), backtest_table() and migration_test() read the weights as frequency weights, so their standard errors count the total weight as the number of observations. fractional_regression() does too, and lgd_table() averages the LGDs with frequency weights, while workout_lgd() and conversion_factor() work on single loans and take no weights. Weights that correct for sampling, such as inverse sampling rates, make these standard errors too small.

  • default_rate_table() counts the weight of the row of each loan at its start month, and central_tendency_shift() and quasi_moment_fit() read their weights as frequency weights, such as the number of obligors of each grade. The default rates and the central tendency of PD calibration count obligors, so weight them for sampling only, never by exposure (Basel CRE36.78).

Periods and thresholds#

  • Periods are ordered as their categories when they are categorical and sorted otherwise. Period 1 of a term structure or an expected credit loss is the first period after its starting point, such as the reporting date, and expected_credit_loss() discounts a default in period \(t\) over \(t\) periods. workout_lgd() discounts a cash flow over the days between the date of default and its own date, divided by 365, and compounds a cost before default in the same way. In survival_table(), an account with the entry \(e\) and the duration \(T\) is at risk in the periods \(e < t \le T\), and an account censored in a period still counts as at risk in it, because its censoring comes after the defaults and exits of the period.

  • A threshold is compared as the rule it implements is worded. A cut-off approves the scores at or below it. significant_increase() flags a PD increase only when it exceeds its thresholds, and assign_stages() moves a loan to stage 2 when it is more than stage_2_days past due and to stage 3 when it is at least stage_3_days past due, as IFRS 9 words its 30-day and 90-day presumptions.

  • The end of delinquency_panel() is the last month observed, given as the month, a date in it or a longer period that ends with it, and every month is observed on its last day, so a payment made on that day counts. An instalment is not past due on its due date, and the status 90+ starts at 90 days past due, as stage 3 does with the stage_3_days of assign_stages().

  • A start month of default_rate_table() is given in the same way, as a period, a date or a string, and stands for its last month, so "2024Q2" is June 2024. Its cohort holds the loans that are not in default at the end of that month, by their grade at that month, and the horizon months after it are the window in which a default counts, so the start month of the calendar year 2025 is December 2024.

Time windows#

  • A feature of kivi.features uses only what is known at the observation time \(t\), so that it is point in time. A window of window_features() is \([t - k, t)\) by default, so an event at \(t\) or later counts in no window; closed="right" gives \((t - k, t]\) for events that are known at their own time, such as balances at the end of a day, which only the caller can judge. A window of panel_features() holds the \(k\) months before the month of \(t\), because the status at the end of that month is not known at \(t\).

  • "3M" counts calendar months, as pandas.DateOffset(months=3) does, so one month before 31 March is 29 February in a leap year, and "90D" counts days. The bounds are written in upper case, and panel_features() takes months only. In the column names the windows are in lower case, such as 3m and 90d.

  • windows is a list, also for one window, as in windows=["3M"]. A pair of bounds with the same unit, the first further back, is the earlier period between them, as ("6M", "3M") is \([t - 6M, t - 3M)\), named 6m_3m. A pair is one window only as an item of the list, as in windows=[("6M", "3M")]: windows=("6M", "3M") is the two windows 6m and 3m.

Errors and warnings#

  • Invalid input raises ValueError, and so does a model that cannot be fitted, such as one with separated classes, which raises kivi.selection.SeparationError. The message names the argument and, where a single value is at fault, shows the value that was received:

>>> bin_edges([1, 2, 3], bins=0)
Traceback (most recent call last):
    ...
ValueError: bins must be a positive integer, got 0
>>> roc_auc([0.1, 0.9, 0.4], [0, 1, 2])

  • A UserWarning means that the result differs from the request, for example fewer bins than requested because of tied values, and says what to change. kivi.univariate.profile_table() records such ties in a column instead, so that profiling many columns raises no warnings.

  • A RuntimeWarning means that a fit stopped before it converged.

  • A FutureWarning announces a change of the public API. Compatibility says when the change takes effect.

  • The wording of the messages may change in any release, so a test that matches a message may need an update; the types of the errors and warnings stay.
