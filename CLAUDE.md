## Architecture

`src/kivi` (src layout, hatchling) is the 0.3 rebuild of the legacy 0.2.x library, reduced to a small NumPy/pandas core. There are no top-level re-exports: the public API is imported from submodules, and each public module has its own page in `docs/api/<module>.rst` listing every entry point.

Fitting edges and grouping are deliberately separate steps:

1. `kivi.binning.bin_edges(values, bins, *, method, special)` fits edges on the reference sample only. Interior duplicate edges are collapsed and the endpoints are always `-inf`/`+inf`, so future out-of-range values still land in a bin (a constant reference yields one bin). The supervised `chimerge_edges`, `tree_edges`, `ks_edges`, `monotonic_edges` and `optimal_edges(values, target, ...)` in the same module return edges of the same form, and so does `variance_edges(values, response, ...)` for a continuous response. ChiMerge, monotonic, optimal and variance binning start from `initial_bins` quantile bins (one bin per distinct value when there are no more distinct values than that, `_fine_positions`, which `_fine_bins` counts); ChiMerge and monotonic binning merge adjacent pairs, and the ChiMerge p-value uses `math.erfc` (one degree of freedom), so there is no scipy dependency. Their `min_bin_share` (1.8, default 0, which keeps every earlier result) merges first, in `_merge_small`: while some fine bin holds less than the share, the smallest (the first of equally small ones) joins the neighbour whose chi-square statistic with it is smaller, the left one on a tie within a relative 1e-10, as `chimerge_groups` does. `monotonic_edges` checks the trend with `_counts.shares`, so `woe_table` with the same `smoothing` sees the same monotonic WOE. `bin_edges` delegates to the private `_edges.fit_edges(..., advice=, stacklevel=)`; `gains_table` (via `_edges.band_edges`), `cutoff_table` and `confusion_table` (both through `strategy._cutoff_values`) call it directly so that the tied-quantile warning points at their caller and suggests fewer bins or explicit edges instead of a `special` argument they do not take. `tree_edges` grows best first and its interior edges are observed values; it matches scikit-learn's `DecisionTreeClassifier(max_leaf_nodes=max_bins, min_samples_leaf=min_bin_share)` splits, which is a useful cross-check but not a dependency. `ks_edges` (Best-KS) cuts each bin at its largest KS among the cuts with `min_bin_share` on both sides, and splits best first by `E_b·N_b·KS_b`, the event/non-event pairs a cut ranks apart (a lower bound of the split's Gini gain), until `max_bins` or until no bin has an allowed cut with a positive KS. Ties within a relative 1e-10 go to the first cut and the first bin (`_first_max`). A side within a relative 1e-10 of the minimum share reaches it, so scaled weights give the edges of repeated rows (`tree_edges` compares exactly, as scikit-learn does), and events and non-events are summed separately (`_cumulative`), so a bin of one class holds exactly zero of the other. With `min_bin_share=0` the gap at its first cut is the statistic of SciPy's `ks_2samp`; the cut is `statistic_location` only where the largest gap is not tied, because `ks_edges` takes the smallest of tied cuts. `optimal_edges(values, target, *, max_bins=5, min_bin_share=0.05, min_bin_events=1, trend="auto", initial_bins=20, special, weights)` (1.8) returns, of all merges of the fine bins, the one with the largest IV whose bins hold at least `min_bin_share` of the fitted weight and at least `min_bin_events` events and as many non-events (weight units, both with a relative 1e-10 of slack, as `ks_edges`; `min_bin_events` must be positive, so every bin has both classes, nothing is smoothed and the IV equals that of `woe_table`) and whose WOE follows `trend`: `increasing` or `decreasing` (every step, `woe_trend`'s rule), `u_shaped` or `inverted_u` (falls then rises, or the reverse; either part may be empty), `any`, or `auto`, the better of the two monotonic trends, increasing on a tie (U shapes only on request). `_optimal` builds the IV and WOE of every candidate bin and hands them to `_best_binning(gain, level, trend, max_bins, *, unit, absolute)`, the dynamic program that both functions share, over tables `best[b][state, i, j]`, the largest gain of rows i..n−1 in b bins whose lowest bin is i..j−1, allocated only for the numbers of bins some binning reaches; `_PREPEND` lists the shape states read from the top bin down, and the gain is summed from the top bin down, so every binning has one float gain. The counts of every range of fine bins come from `_range_sums`, which adds the aligned power-of-two blocks inside the range (a segment tree) instead of differencing cumulative sums, so a weight of 1e16 cannot round away the weights of 1 next to it. `_steps` counts a step as level within a relative 1e-10 of the larger level or 1e-10 × `unit` (1 for a WOE); gains tie within a relative 1e-10 or `absolute` (`_GAIN_TIE` = 1e-20 for the IV; `_floor`, `_chosen`, also between the two trends of `auto`), and of tied binnings the one with fewer bins wins, then the smaller lowest edge, then the next, which the reconstruction from the lowest bin up against the floor of the largest gain applies exactly (`_reaches`). A single bin always qualifies, so it raises only when `min_bin_events` exceeds the events or non-events; time is O(n³k) and memory O(n²k). `tests/test_supervised_binning.py` compares it with an exhaustive search over every partition of the fine bins (about 600 cases, unweighted and weighted) and hard-codes 50 results of optbinning 1.1.0 (five bank columns × five trends × two constraint sets, with kivi's fine bins as `user_splits` moved by `nextafter` because optbinning cuts `x < s`; not a test dependency, because it needs Python ≥ 3.11 and ortools NumPy ≥ 2.0.2): the edges agree and the IV to 1e-12, except that optbinning's presolve with `min_event_rate_diff=0` misses the increasing optimum of `age`, which a separate test documents. `variance_edges(values, response, *, max_bins=5, min_bin_share=0.05, trend="auto", initial_bins=20, special, weights)` (1.8) runs the same dynamic program (`_between`) on the weighted within-bin sum of squares of a finite real `response` that may leave [0, 1], such as a realized LGD or CCF (missing values, infinities and booleans raise; missing and special `values` are left out with their responses): it maximizes the between-bin sum B = Σ W_b (m_b − m)², each bin adding S_b²/W_b with S_b its weighted sum of the response centred on the overall mean, so no second moment is subtracted from another. The response is first scaled by a power of two, which changes no partition, so responses from 1e-300 to 1e300 neither underflow nor overflow. `trend` constrains the bin means (level within a relative 1e-10 or 1e-10 × the RMS of the response), binnings tie within a relative 1e-10 of B or 1e-20 × Σ w y², so equal means never split, and `auto` prefers increasing on a tie; there is no `min_bin_events` and no `smoothing`. It has no `fit_spec` method, because a `BinningSpec` holds WOE tables: the caller groups with `cut` and summarizes with `lgd.lgd_table`, as the LGD guide does for the loan-to-value ratio. `tests/test_supervised_binning.py` compares it with an exhaustive search (about 700 cases), and `test_reference.py` with scikit-learn's `DecisionTreeRegressor(max_depth=1)` stump, comparing partitions rather than thresholds.
2. The caller applies the same edges to every sample with `kivi.binning.cut(series, edges, special=...)`, or `pd.cut` when there are no special values. `special` codes (e.g. bank `pdays == -1`) are left out of the fit in step 1 (the supervised functions drop those rows together with their labels in `_labeled`), and `cut` gives each its own category ahead of the intervals; without `special` it returns exactly `pd.cut`'s result. `_special.special_positions` matches the codes by equality, and values that are not numbers also after `pd.to_numeric`, so a text `"-1"` gets the code `-1`; boolean codes raise because `True == 1`. Categorical variables skip steps 1–2 and are passed as-is, or are grouped first: `category_groups(values, *, min_share, min_count, rare_label, special, weights)` pools rare categories, and `chimerge_groups(values, target, *, max_groups, alpha, min_group_share, smoothing, special, weights)` sorts the categories by smoothed odds and merges adjacent ones with the ChiMerge statistic, so the groups come out in increasing WOE order. Ties go to first appearance (category order for categoricals), with statistics within a relative 1e-10 counted as equal. Both return a plain dict category → group; a merged group's label joins at most five categories with `", "` and then `"... (+n)"`. `group_categories(values, groups, *, unseen="raise")` applies it like `cut`, and `unseen` may name a group for new categories.
3. `kivi.woe.woe_table(groups, target)` and `kivi.stability.psi_table(reference, current)` take already-grouped series and return one row per group. Totals are the caller's `.sum()` of the `iv` / `psi` column. `kivi.woe.woe_trend(table)` (a `woe_table`, a `BinningSpec` table or a Series of WOE values, through `_validation.woe_values`) names the shape of the `woe` of the rows labelled with a `pd.Interval`, in table order, so special and missing rows are ignored: neighbours within a relative 1e-10 (`_TIE`) neither rise nor fall, and the remaining steps give `flat` (none, or one interval row), `increasing`, `decreasing`, `u_shaped` (falls, then rises), `inverted_u` or `irregular` (more than one change). The six labels are a `typing.Literal` and public; a table without an interval row raises.
4. `kivi.woe.woe_transform(groups, table, unseen="raise")` maps groups back to the fitted WOE (the scorecard encoding). It factorizes the groups once, looks each distinct label up in the table by equality (intervals from the same edges match), and gives missing values the table's missing-group WOE. A label absent from the table raises unless `unseen` is a number, so new categories and empty development intervals never turn into silent NaN.

Feature engineering comes before these steps, and the modeling modules build on them:

- **`kivi.features`** (1.3): point-in-time features of event data and of monthly status panels.
  - **`window_features(events, observations, *, id, time, observation_time, windows=("3M", "6M", "12M"), aggregations=None, ratios=None, closed="left", prefix="")`** aggregates the events of each entity over `[t − k, t)` windows, or `(t − k, t]` with `closed="right"`. `"3M"` counts calendar months by `DateOffset`, `"90D"` local calendar days; a bound is exactly `[1-9][0-9]*[MD]`. A pair is one window only nested in the list, `windows=[("6M", "3M")]` (one unit, first bound further back); a top-level `("6M", "3M")` is two windows, and `windows="3M"` raises. The aggregations are `count`, `sum`, `mean`, `min`, `max`, `std`, `nunique`, `first`, `last` and `days_since_last` (elapsed fractional days from the observation time, also for a pair); `aggregations=None` gives only the `events_{w}` counts. Missing values are left out of their column's aggregations, while `events_{w}` counts rows; booleans count as 0/1, and the numeric aggregations reject text and ±inf.
  - **Columns.** Per window `{prefix}events_{w}`, then `{prefix}{column}_{aggregation}_{w}` column by column and aggregation by aggregation, and the ratios last: `{numerator}_over_{w}` when the two differ only in their window, else `{numerator}_over_{denominator}`. Ratios name output columns, prefix included, and are NaN for a 0 or NaN denominator. Column names and the prefix keep their case; only kivi's tokens, the aggregation names and the window labels (`3m`, `90d`, `6m_3m`), are lower case. `events`, `count` and `nunique` are int64 and the rest float64; an empty window gives 0 for `count`, `sum` and `nunique` and NaN otherwise. The result keeps the index and row order of `observations`.
  - **Computation.** The events are sorted once (stable `lexsort` on entity, time, row) and every bound is a `searchsorted` on composite keys. Counts come from cumulative counts, and `sum`/`mean` add the aligned power-of-two blocks that lie inside each window (`_blocks`/`_block_sums`), so a large earlier value never rounds away the small values of a window. `min`, `max`, `std` (two-pass) and `nunique` `reduceat` over the window rows, expanded in chunks of `_CHUNK` = 2^20 rows.
  - **Times.** Time columns must have a datetime64 dtype (strings raise; unlike `delinquency_panel`, nothing is parsed) and are compared as int64 nanoseconds. Events and observations are both tz-naive or in one zone (compared by `str(tz)`), else the call raises. Bounds are computed on the local calendar and localized back with `nonexistent="shift_forward"` and the first occurrence in the repeated hour; events are compared as instants. Ids are matched by equality (`Index.get_indexer`), so `1` and `"1"` differ, and a missing id or time raises.
  - **`panel_features(panel, observations, *, id, month, status, observation_time, windows=("6M", "12M"), bad="30-59", prefix="")`** uses the month-end statuses of the months before the observation month (month windows only, days raise; pairs nested as above), with the worst status per entity and month. `status` must be an ordered categorical with `bad` (a string or an integer) among its categories; `month` holds monthly periods or datetimes, which stand for their month. Months and observation times each use their own calendar, so tz-aware ones in two zones raise. Per window: `months` (the months with a status), `bad_months`, `longest_bad_run` and `longest_good_run` (int64; months without a status count nowhere and end runs, and a run is clipped to the window) and `worst_status` (the status dtype, missing for an empty window); then `months_since_bad` (float, over the whole history, NaN if never bad). It has no `closed` and no `ratios`.
  - **`FeatureSpec(id, observation_time, events=(), ratios=(), panel=(), kivi_version=None)`** is frozen; its groups are read-only mappings with tuples, and `dataclasses.replace` validates again. An `events` group holds the arguments of one `window_features` call plus `table` (default `"events"`), a `panel` group those of one `panel_features` call plus `table` (default `"panel"`). `events` and `panel` default to `()`, None and JSON `null` are rejected, and a spec needs at least one group; one `id` column serves every table. Ratios may combine groups but not divide a `worst_status`. `columns` lists the output; `compute(observations, *, events=None, panel=None)` takes a DataFrame when every group of that kind reads one table, else a dict with exactly the table names, and requires each argument exactly when there are groups of that kind. Columns: window features, panel features, ratios. Equality and hash compare the JSON text, which includes `kivi_version`; pickling goes through `to_dict`; `repr` is `FeatureSpec(columns=[...])`.
  - **JSON.** `to_dict()` holds `format` (`kivi.FeatureSpec`), `format_version` and `kivi_version`, then the constructor's arguments as it takes them, with every group key, lists for tuples and `aggregations` as `{column: [aggregations]}` in output order, so `FeatureSpec(**those keys) == spec`. Format version 1 is final with 1.3.0 (there is no `format_version` property); `from_dict` requires exactly these keys, `from_json` rejects a repeated key in any object (`object_pairs_hook`), and a newer version raises. `tests/data/feature_spec_1.3.0.json` and `.features.json` were written by running `tests/test_features.py` as a script and must not change. `tests/test_features.py` also holds the brute-force references, the leakage tests and the pandas `groupby().rolling()` cross-check.
- **`kivi.selection`**:
  - Functions: `correlation_filter` (Pearson or Spearman correlation through `method`, computed with `DataFrame.corr`), `vif`, `vif_filter`, `logistic_regression`, `stepwise`, `selection_path` and the exception `SeparationError`. `selection.py` imports the public `kivi.metrics` (no cycle).
  - **Fitting.** `_logit` fits by IRLS on centred, scaled columns and maps the coefficients and covariance back to the original scale. Wald p-values use `math.erfc`. Since 1.6 `_logit.fit` also takes fractional targets (gradient Xᵀw(y − p), the quasi-log-likelihood of Papke and Wooldridge; for 0/1 labels the residual `y·P(0) − (1 − y)·P(1)` gives bit-identical fits) and `robust=True` gives the HC0 sandwich A⁻¹ (Σ w (y − p)² x xᵀ) A⁻¹. Only exact 0/1 targets can be separated: a fractional target bounds the linear predictor. The design-matrix checks that `selection` and `lgd` share live in `_logit` (`as_frame`, `with_intercept`, `check_not_constant`, `check_independent`, `singular_column`, `warn_not_converged`), called as `_logit.<name>`.
  - **Separation.** It is reported only with a certificate: some fitted probabilities sit at 0 or 1, and the Newton steps move every row towards its own class.
  - **`vif`.** It includes an intercept, computed from one SVD of the standardized columns (`_vif`). Its index, like that of `logistic_regression`, is named `variable`.
  - **`vif_filter(X, *, threshold=5.0, priority=None)`.** Each round computes `_vif` of the kept columns and drops the largest unless it is at most `threshold` (a finite number above 1); it stops at one column and returns the kept labels as an Index in the order of `X` (positions for arrays), like `correlation_filter`. VIFs within a relative 1e-10 of the largest tie (infinite VIFs first); ties drop the lowest `priority` (a Series by label, else by position), then the LAST in column order, so earlier columns are kept as in `correlation_filter`. No weights.
  - **`selection_path(X, target, *, order=None, validation=None, weights=None)`.** Row k (index `step`, 1..k) is the fit of the first k entries of `order` (labels, positions for arrays, a subset allowed) with the same design and `_fit` as `logistic_regression`, so the coefficients are identical: `variable`, `auc`, `gini`, `ks`, `max_p_value`, `min_coefficient` (both without the intercept), then with `validation=(X_valid, target_valid)` `validation_auc`, `validation_gini`, `validation_ks` (X_valid matched by label for a DataFrame `X`, by position otherwise; each validation row counts once). The metrics score the linear predictor summed column by column, not a matrix product, so identical rows stay tied on every CPU. Separation raises `SeparationError` naming the step, and non-convergence warns with the step; there is no early stop.
  - **`stepwise(positive=True)`.** Every coefficient of the selected model stays positive, as WOE inputs require.
  - **Weights.** `logistic_regression` and `stepwise` take frequency `weights` (statsmodels `GLM(freq_weights=)` semantics): integer weights equal repeated rows, rows with zero weight are dropped before `_logit.fit`, and the constant and collinearity checks run on the rows with positive weight.
- **`kivi.scorecard`**:
  - **Scaling.** `score_scaling` gives factor = pdo / ln 2 and offset = base_score − factor·ln(base_odds), with the odds as good:bad.
  - **Conversion and points.** `pd_to_score` and `score_to_pd` are exact inverses. `points_table` builds the card from WOE tables and coefficients, and `woe_to_points` scores new rows.
  - **`reason_codes`.** Costs within `atol` of zero don't count, and every points value must match a group of the table. `unseen_points` (one value or one per variable) exempts only points that match no group and lie within `atol` of it, the points that `woe_transform(unseen=...)` gave unseen groups; every other mismatch still raises.
- **`kivi.expert`**: AHP weights for expert scorecards.
  - `ahp_weights(matrix, *, method="eigenvector", rtol=1e-3)` gives the weights (Series `weight`, index `factor`, positive, summing to 1) of a pairwise comparison matrix by Saaty's principal eigenvector or by the row geometric means (`"geometric"`, the weights of the legacy `AHP` class). The two coincide for every reciprocal matrix of up to three factors.
  - `ahp_consistency(matrix, *, rtol)` gives `lambda_max` (clipped to at least n), `ci`, `ri` and `cr` as an unnamed Series with the index `statistic`. The random index is Tone's table for up to 15 factors (`_RANDOM_INDEX`; more factors raise), a constant that changes only as a fix (`compatibility.rst`), and `ci`, `ri` and `cr` are 0 for n ≤ 2. The CR rule of 0.1 is advice in the guide, never an error or a warning.
  - `combine_weights(parent, children)` multiplies the weights of factors and sub-factors on a (`factor`, `sub_factor`) MultiIndex, in the order of `parent` and then of each child. It needs a `children` entry for every factor and no other key, checks that each set of weights sums to 1 to a relative 1e-6 and joins two levels; deeper hierarchies are combined level by level.
  - `_comparison_matrix` validates the matrix: a DataFrame with the same flat, distinct labels on its index and columns, or an array or nested list numbered from 0; finite, positive entries; a diagonal of 1 and reciprocal entries to the keyword `rtol` (default 1e-3, which accepts reciprocals rounded to four decimals). The matrix is used as given.
- **`kivi.metrics`**: `roc_auc`, `gini`, `ks`, `gains_table`, `brier_score`, `roc_table`, `roc_auc_interval`, `delong_test`, `generalized_auc` and `expected_maximum_profit`.
  - They expect a score that rises with the event probability, so pass a PD or `-points`.
  - `ks` is the absolute largest gap.
  - `gains_table(score, target, *, bins)` takes an integer, which fits bands with `bin_edges` so that ties warn in the same way, or explicit edges. It keeps empty bands, so tables of two samples line up.
  - Every metric takes `weights`; score and label checks are shared through `_validation.scores_and_labels`. Integer weights equal repeated rows exactly, for `gains_table` only on fixed edges (an integer `bins` uses weighted inverted-CDF quantiles).
  - `roc_table(score, target)` has one row per distinct score, riskiest first, after an `inf` row that flags nobody: `cum_non_event, cum_event, false_positive_rate, true_positive_rate, precision`, the rows of `sklearn` `roc_curve(drop_intermediate=False)`. Its trapezoid area is `roc_auc` and its largest gap `ks`.
  - `roc_auc_interval(score, target, *, level=0.95)` (rows `auc` and `gini` in an index named `statistic`, columns `estimate, se, lower, upper`) and `delong_test(current, new, target, *, level)` (difference = new − current, with `se`, `z`, `p_value`) use DeLong's variance from placement values computed with midrank counts (Sun & Xu 2014). Their weights are frequency weights, as in `logistic_regression`, so inverse sampling rates give too small an SE. The interval is normal, clipped to [0, 1] (or [−1, 1]), and `_auc` is clipped to [0, 1].
  - `generalized_auc(estimated, realized, *, level=0.95, weights=None)` is the ECB's gAUC (annex 3.2) of the contingency table of two ordered segmentations (ordered categoricals by category, numbers by value; unordered categoricals, booleans and missing values raise): rows `gauc`, `somers_d` (index `statistic`), columns as `roc_auc_interval`. D(C|R) = (P − Q) / w_r equals SciPy's `somersd(estimated, realized)`; the SE is the ECB's s for `gauc` and 2s for `somers_d`, and it is 0 for a degenerate table (one realized segment, or a perfect or reversed ranking). The current-vs-initial tests of the AUC and gAUC are guide recipes, not functions.
  - `expected_maximum_profit(score, target, *, no_loss_share=0.55, full_loss_share=0.1, roi=0.2644, weights=None)` (1.7) is the EMP of Verbraken, Bravo, Weber and Baesens (EJOR 238(2), 2014): a Series (index `statistic`: `emp`, `rejected_share`). The loss λ of a default is 0 with probability `no_loss_share`, 1 with `full_loss_share` and uniform in between; the integral is in closed form over the vertices of the upper hull of the cumulative (good, bad) counts of the distinct scores, rejected from the highest down (`_upper_hull`, a monotone chain that drops collinear points), so ties are rejected together and only the order of the scores matters. Vertex i is best for λ from ROI·ΔG/ΔD of its segment to that of the next, and the term of `full_loss_share` sits at the last vertex with λ_i < 1, as in the R package `EMP` 2.0.6 and empulse 0.14.0 (Eq. 15 of the paper puts it one vertex later, an erratum). Weights are frequency weights, rescaled by a power of two first. `tests/test_metrics.py` hard-codes values computed once with empulse (not a test dependency: it needs Python ≥ 3.11 and NumPy ≥ 1.25.2) and checks a brute-force integration over a grid of λ.
- **`kivi.calibration`**: `prior_correction`, `corrected_intercept`, `calibration_table`, `binomial_test`, `jeffreys_test`, `hosmer_lemeshow`, `spiegelhalter_test`, `brier_decomposition`, `herfindahl_index`, `concentration_test`, `backtest_table`, `platt_fit`/`platt_predict`, `isotonic_fit`/`isotonic_predict`, `central_tendency_shift` and `quasi_moment_fit`.
  - `calibration_table` counts through `_counts.group_counts`, takes `weights` and returns `total, event, event_rate, mean_probability`.
  - `platt_fit` is a one-variable `_logit` fit and raises `SeparationError` like `logistic_regression`. `isotonic_fit` runs pool-adjacent-violators and returns edges from `-inf` to `inf` plus one probability per band, which `isotonic_predict(score, edges, probability)` applies; a score between two bands gets the upper band. The fit functions return plain numbers and the predict functions apply them, as `bin_edges` and `cut` do.
  - **Central tendency (1.8, Tasche 2009 and 2013, EBA/GL/2017/16 section 5.3; `docs/design/kivi-1.8-default-rates.md` holds the source check and nine decisions).** `central_tendency_shift(probability, *, central_tendency, weights)` returns the float Δ at which the weighted mean of σ(logit p + Δ) equals the target (Tasche's scaled likelihood ratio, EBA's TTC B/C), found by `_log_odds_shift` with Newton steps safeguarded by bisection to adjacent floats; PDs of 0 and 1 stay as they are and bound the reachable targets, which raise beyond them. The caller applies it as `p / (p + (1 - p) * exp(-shift))`, `intercept + shift` or points minus `factor * shift`. `quasi_moment_fit(score, *, central_tendency, accuracy_ratio, weights)` returns `(slope, intercept)` for `platt_predict`: the logistic curve of a score rising with the risk whose mean PD is the central tendency and whose implied accuracy ratio (`_accuracy_ratio`, ties as half, as `metrics.gini` counts them on the implied sample of w·p events and w·(1 − p) non-events) is the target; the intercept is solved per slope like the shift, and the slope by `_regula_falsi` (Illinois steps with bisection, to adjacent floats) from the binormal start of Tasche's A.7. A target at or above the AR of the steepest curve (`_step_probabilities`) raises and names that limit; AR = 0 gives slope 0. Both take frequency weights and raise for missing values. `tests/test_calibration.py` hard-codes Tasche's (2013) S&P counts: `gini` reproduces table 4, `quasi_moment_fit` on the robust-logit scores table 5 (15 of 17 grades to the printed digits, BB and CCC-C within 0.0015 pp, listed), and table 7's invariant-AR and scaled-likelihood-ratio columns within 0.0005 and 0.0004 pp.
  - **Validation tests (1.4, the ECB's instructions for reporting the validation of IRB models, February 2019).** `spiegelhalter_test(probability, target, *, weights)` returns `(statistic, pvalue)` like `hosmer_lemeshow`; z ∝ Brier score minus its expectation, so with every probability below 1/2 a positive z means more events than predicted and a negative one fewer, both rejections; p is two-sided. `brier_decomposition(groups, target, probability, *, weights)` is a Series (index `statistic`) `brier, reliability, resolution, uncertainty, within_variance, within_covariance` with brier = REL − RES + UNC + WBV − WBC exactly (Murphy 1973; Stephenson et al. 2008); the last two are 0 when each group has one probability. `concentration_test(counts, *, initial_counts=None, initial_cv=None)` (counts of every grade, empty ones included; two Series must share an index) gives `hi` = 1 + ln(HHI)/ln K, `cv`, `initial_hi`, `initial_cv`, `statistic`, `p_value` (1 − Φ(S)); neither initial argument gives NaN for the last four, both raise, and `initial_cv` above √(K − 1) raises. `backtest_table(groups, realized, estimated, *, weights)` (index `bin`: `total, estimated, realized, difference, variance, statistic, p_value`) is the one-sided paired t test (p = 1 − t_{N−1}(T)) of ECB 2.6.2.1/2.8.2.1/2.9.3.1/2.9.3.2; the ELBE test of 2.7.2.1 is two-sided, `2 * min(p_value, 1 - p_value)`, documented rather than an option. `_group_means` shifts each group by its first value of positive weight, so equal differences give a variance of exactly 0 and T = ±inf; N ≤ 1 (total weight ≤ 1) gives NaN. `realized` and `estimated` are finite reals that may leave [0, 1]; booleans and missing values raise.
  - The distribution functions live in the private `_stats`: `betainc`, `gammaincc`, `chi2_sf`, `normal_quantile`, `normal_cdf` (arrays, `0.5 erfc(-x / √2)`) and `student_t_sf(t, dof)`. `student_t_sf` uses I_x(df/2, 1/2) / 2 in the tails and 1/2 − I_y(1/2, df/2) / 2 near the centre, choosing the argument below the beta mean; from `_T_EXPANSION_DOF` = 100 degrees of freedom and ln(1 + t²/df) ≤ 1 it uses `_t_tail_expansion` instead, ½ R erfc(√z) [1 + Σ g_k m^{−2k} Γ(2k + ½, z)/Γ(½, z)] with m = df/2 − ¼, z = m ln(1 + t²/df) and the 13 coefficients of (v / (2 sinh(v/2)))^{1/2}, because the continued fractions lose about log10(df) digits near the beta mean (8e-8 at 1e9). The normal CDF is `math.erfc` where scalars need it (`metrics`, `_logit`, `binning`). For `kivi.capital` (1.5) `_stats` adds `beta_quantile(a, b, probability)` (Newton from the Numerical Recipes start, safeguarded by bisection that is geometric in x below ½ and in 1 − x above, with the residual taken in the tail that `betainc` computes without forming 1 − x; closed forms for a = 1 or b = 1; a quantile below the smallest normal number returns that number), `bivariate_normal_cdf(x, y, rho)` (Genz 2004: Plackett's identity by 30-point Gauss–Legendre from ρ = 0 for |ρ| < 0.925 and from ρ = ±1 with the closed-form series terms above; absolute error below 2e-16) and an array `normal_quantile` (NaN for missing values, each distinct value computed once by the scalar code, so elements equal scalar calls). `_log_front` (the Stirling branch for both shapes ≥ 10) writes a ln(x/p0) − (total x − a) instead of a·log1pmx((x − p0)/p0) once x < p0/2, and likewise for 1 − x, because x − p0 rounds to −p0 once x/p0 < 1e-16 and `betainc` then returned 0 (fixed in 1.5, before release).
  - **Master scale.** `master_scale(probability, *, boundary="geometric", labels)` returns one row per grade (`lower`, `probability`, `upper`, index named `grade`) covering [0, 1]. Without `labels`, the grades are the index of a Series `probability`, otherwise 1 to n; the bounds are geometric (or arithmetic) means of adjacent PDs. `fit_grade_pd(event, total, *, smoothing)` fits ln(rate) linearly in the grade number by least squares weighted with `total`; zero-default grades get `smoothing` defaults, empty grades stay out of the fit but get a PD. `assign_grades(probability, scale)` equals `pd.cut(probability, [0, *upper], labels=scale.index, include_lowest=True)` as an ordered categorical, so `calibration_table` counts it in scale order.

A group without points raises unless `unseen_points` is given.

Appended after these (1.1): `skewness` and `kurtosis` (pandas' bias-corrected `skew()`/`kurt()`; with weights, frequency-weight formulas with n = total weight, so integer weights equal repeated rows) and `cv` = std / mean (NaN for a zero mean) of the numeric values, and with a target `ks` (the KS of the numeric values, as `metrics.ks`) and `chi2_p_value` (the chi-squared test of independence between the target and the groups of the quick binning, missing and special groups included, df = groups − 1, through `_stats.chi2_sf`). It shares the column helpers in `_validation` with `kivi.spec`.
- **`kivi.strategy`**: scores rise with risk, and a cut-off approves scores at or below it (ties approved).
  - `cutoff_table(score, target, *, cutoffs, loss, revenue, weights)` has one row per cut-off; an integer `cutoffs` uses the `bin_edges` edges above the lowest, so the rows add up the bands of `gains_table(bins=n)`. `loss` and `revenue` (1.7) are finite, non-negative amounts per row (`_amounts`) that append `approved_loss`, `approved_revenue` and, with both, `profit` = `approved_revenue` − `approved_loss`, so costs go into `loss`.
  - `confusion_table(score, target, *, cutoffs, weights)` has the rows of `cutoff_table` for the same `cutoffs` (index `cutoff`): a decline (score above the cut-off) is the positive prediction and an event the positive class, giving `true_positive`, `false_positive`, `true_negative`, `false_negative`, `precision` (NaN when nothing is declined), `recall` (equal to `rejected_event_share`), `f1` and `accuracy`.
  - `swap_set(current, new, target=None, *, current_cutoff, new_cutoff, weights)` returns four rows indexed (`current`, `new`) in {approve, decline}²; a missing target is an unknown outcome that counts in `total` but not in `known`/`event`.
  - `expected_loss(probability, lgd, ead)` broadcasts and names a Series result `expected_loss`; `expected_loss_table(groups, probability, lgd, ead, *, weights)` counts groups with `_counts.group_counts` and maps rows back with `_counts.group_rows`.
  - Booleans count as 0 and 1 in `probability`, as in `kivi.metrics` and `kivi.calibration`, but not in `lgd`, `ead`, `loss`, `revenue` or `weights`.
- **`kivi.rules`** (1.7): policy rules; `docs/design/kivi-1.7-rules-profit.md` holds the EMP source check and the six decisions of 1.7, and `docs/design/kivi-1.8-roadmap.md` §3.4 the rule search of 1.8.
  - **Inputs.** `rules` must be a DataFrame with unique, single-level column names, one column per rule of booleans or 0 and 1 (`_validation.as_flags`), True where the rule hits. A missing value in a rule raises (decide with `fillna`). `target` is optional, and its missing labels are unknown outcomes (`_validation.known_outcomes`, as in `swap_set`) that count in the hits and `approved` but not in `known`, `event` or the rates. Series must have the index of `rules` (`shared_index(..., frames=True)`).
  - **`rule_table(rules, target=None, *, weights=None)`** evaluates each rule on every row: index `rule`, columns `hits, hit_rate, unique_hits` (hit by no other rule), and with a target `known, event, event_rate, lift, recall, unique_event`, rates over known outcomes.
  - **`rule_set_table(rules, target=None, *, weights=None)`** applies the columns in order, as a decision waterfall: `new_hits` (first hit by this rule), with a target `new_event`, then `approved, approval_rate`, and with a target the `cutoff_table` columns `approved_event, approved_event_rate, rejected_event_share, rejected_non_event_share` of the book left approved. With every outcome known, the cumulative columns of a row equal those of `cutoff_table(declined, target, cutoffs=[0])`, and those of the last row do not depend on the order.
  - Weights are frequency weights: counts become sums of weights, and integer weights equal repeated rows. The guide uses `make_applications(rng=0)`, where `high_debt | young` equals `policy_decline` and adding `incumbent_pd >= 0.15` as a third rule approves exactly `approved`.
  - **`rule_candidates(frame, target, *, columns=None, method="tree", max_bins=5, min_hit_rate=0.01, min_lift=2.0, special=None, weights=None)`** (1.8) fits cut points per numeric column with the public `binning.tree_edges` or `ks_edges` (`rules.py` imports no private helper of `binning`) with `min_bin_share = min(min_hit_rate, 0.5)`, so risky tails below the default 5% can be found, on the rows with a known outcome, an observed value that is not a `special` code (column → codes) and a positive weight. Each interior edge t gives `> t` and `<= t`, and the side with the higher event rate (`>` on a tie) is the candidate; candidates with `hit_rate >= min_hit_rate` and `lift >= min_lift` are kept, sorted by `lift` descending, ties by column and then threshold order. Index `rule` (`"age <= 25.0"`), columns `variable, operator, threshold, hits, hit_rate, known, event, event_rate, lift`, a subset of `rule_table`'s in its order; an empty table keeps the dtypes. Missing values and special codes hit no rule, and the counts equal `rule_table` of the full rule form `((frame[v] > t) & ~frame[v].isin(codes)).fillna(False)` (or `<=`), also with weights and nullable dtypes; `lift` divides by the event rate of all known outcomes. Categorical and boolean columns are skipped by default and raise when named; a column without both classes among its fitted rows warns and is skipped by default and raises when named. Counts are computed per candidate, without an n × m boolean frame.
- **`kivi.reject_inference`**: `fuzzy_augmentation(probability, *, factor)` returns two rows per reject (bad then good, weights min(k·p, 1) and its complement, index repeated); `parceling(score, target, reject_score, *, bins, factor, expected, rng)` labels the rejects of each band at the accepts' bad rate times `factor`, with a local generator (or the expected weights); `reweighting(score, reject_score, *, bins)` weights each accept by (accepts + rejects) / accepts of its band. Bands are pooled quantiles of both samples; a band with rejects but no accepts raises. With `factor=1` and the same encoding, a fuzzy refit returns the accepts-only coefficients exactly. The weights make the standard errors of a refit too small; the docs say so.
- **`kivi.spec`**: `fit_spec(frame, target, *, columns, method, bins, special, options, unseen, smoothing, weights)` fits every column through the binning functions (`method` `"quantile"`, `"uniform"`, `"chimerge"`, `"tree"`, `"ks"`, `"monotonic"` or, from 1.8, `"optimal"` for numeric columns, each with the options of its function), `cut` and `woe_table`, and returns a frozen `BinningSpec`. The methods `"rare"` and `"chimerge"` group a categorical column with `category_groups`/`chimerge_groups`; `BinningSpec.groups` holds the read-only mappings, and `unseen` for a grouped column may be `{"group": label}`.
  - Numeric dtypes other than bool are binned; everything else is categorical (an object column holding only numbers warns). Encoding raises when a column fitted as numeric arrives with a non-numeric dtype, so special codes are never silently missed.
  - The edges come back as read-only copies and the tables as read-only mappings. Equality and the hash compare the cached JSON, which includes `kivi_version`.
  - `to_json`/`from_json` write strict JSON (`format` `kivi.BinningSpec`, `format_version` 1, or 2 when a column is grouped; `BinningSpec.FORMAT_VERSION` is the newest version it reads; infinities as `{"float": "inf"}`). A spec without groupings writes exactly the version-1 file of 0.7.0. Numeric table rows store the exact interval edges, and `from_dict` maps them back to the `pd.cut` labels, so the file never depends on pandas' label rounding. A newer `format_version` is rejected. `tests/data/binning_spec_0.6.0.json` (format 1) and `binning_spec_0.8.0.json` (format 2, a `"rare"` and a `"chimerge"` grouping, a `{"group": ...}` unseen) were written by the code of those tags, next to their `woe_transform` of a fixed frame (`*.woe.json`); the docstring of `tests/test_spec_compat.py` has the commands that wrote them. Loading a file and encoding with it are checked exactly: they back the promise that later releases read old files, so a change that breaks them needs a new format version instead. A fresh fit is compared with the files to a relative 1e-12, because NumPy rounds the last digit of a logarithm differently on CPUs with and without AVX-512 and the CI runners vary; that comparison is a parity guard, and a deliberate fix of fitted results may update it without a new format version.
- **`kivi.vintage`**:
  - **`delinquency_panel(loan, due_date, amount_due, payment_loan, payment_date, payment_amount, *, end, origination=None, principal=None, tolerance=0.0)`** builds the loan × month panel from instalment schedules and payments (four schedule inputs on one index, three payment inputs on another, like the rejects of `parceling`). Its columns follow `make_loan_panel`: `loan_id, cohort, month, month_on_book, days_past_due, overdue_amount, outstanding`, `balance` with `principal`, and `status` last, on a RangeIndex sorted by loan and month.
  - **Rows.** Each loan is observed at every month end from the month of its first due date to `end`, which is required (it is the data cut-off, so a loan that stopped paying never ends early without warning). The last day of the month counts, and a string `end` is read as `pandas.Period`, so `"2024Q1"` ends in March; numbers and booleans raise. A loan leaves after the month end at which every instalment counts as paid (prepayment included) and keeps rolling past maturity until `end`, as in `make_loan_panel`; a loan first due after `end` has no rows.
  - **Allocation.** At each month end only the payments made by then count. They go to the oldest unpaid instalment first (same-day instalments in schedule order), so prepayments count towards later instalments, and amounts beyond the total due or after `end` are not used (no credit balance). Instalments due on the same day add up. Instalment k counts as paid when the instalments up to k lack at most `tolerance` together (`C_k - P_t <= tolerance`, with a relative 1e-10 slack for float sums); the remainder stays owed, so repeated shortfalls add up, and a `tolerance` as large as the total due lets an unpaid loan leave in its first month.
  - **Columns.** `days_past_due` runs from the due date of the oldest instalment that does not count as paid to the month end (0 on the due date), and `status` puts it in the buckets of `make_loan_panel` by days, `current`, `1-29`, `30-59`, `60-89`, `90+` (90 days or more), so an instalment due on the 31st can skip a bucket. `overdue_amount` and `outstanding` are exact unpaid amounts, and `balance` pays interest before principal within an instalment. `cohort` is the month before the first due date, or with `origination` (a Series indexed by loan or a mapping, covering every loan) the origination month, and `month_on_book = month - cohort`.
  - **Inputs.** Payments of loans not in the schedule raise, dates go through `pandas.to_datetime` (numeric and period dtypes raise, the time of day is dropped, tz-aware dates keep their local date), and running sums are per loan (`groupby().cumsum()`). There are no `weights`: the counting functions take them.
  - **Migrations (ECB 2.5.5.1–2.5.5.2).** `migration_bandwidth(before, after, *, grades, weights)` (Series, index `statistic`: `upper`, `lower`) and `migration_test(before, after, *, grades, weights)` (MultiIndex (`before`, `after`), one row per off-diagonal cell: `total` = N_i, `share` = p_ij, `statistic`, `p_value` = Φ(z)) take raw grades like `transition_matrix`, with `grades` from the best to the worst (at least two, distinct). A status in `after` outside `grades` (`"default"`, `"closed"`) counts only in N_i; missing values and `before` outside `grades` raise. The MWB normalizer is the furthest move from the grade in either direction, max(|i − K|, |i − 1|). NaN where the ECB leaves the field empty: an empty grade, both shares 0, or one share 1.
  - **`default_rate_table(loan, month, grade, defaulted, *, horizon=12, starts=None, probability=None, weights=None)`** (1.8) is the cohort approach of EBA/GL/2017/16 ¶73–78 and ECB 2019 §2.3: index (`start`, `grade`), columns `total, event, exited, event_rate`, and `mean_probability` with `probability`. A loan in default at a start month belongs to no cohort, its grade is the one of the start row, and a default in any of the `horizon` months after it counts once per loan; loans that leave the panel stay in `total` and those without a default count in `exited` (¶76), unlike the Kaplan–Meier censoring of `survival_table`, and a loan without a row in some month counts as not in default in it. Months are monthly periods or datetimes on their own calendar (`_panel_months`); `starts` are read like the `end` of `delinquency_panel` (`_end_month`, `"2024Q2"` is June) and default to the quarter ends whose window lies in the panel (¶78), and an explicit start with an incomplete window raises. Grades follow the categories of a categorical and are sorted otherwise, with a missing grade as its own last row (¶75; the ECB leaves it out). It raises for a missing loan, month or flag and for two rows of a loan in one month, checks `probability` only on cohort rows, and counts the weight of each loan's start row. Tested against a brute force on random panels with gaps, exits and repeated defaults, `calibration_table` for one start, 1 − Kaplan–Meier of `survival_table` for a panel without exits, and repeated loans for integer weights.
  - `vintage_table(loan, cohort, month_on_book, bad, *, weights)` gives the cumulative ever-bad rate per cohort and month on book. Months a cohort has not reached, and months before its first observed month on book, are NaN, never 0. `transition_matrix(before, after, *, states, weights)` and `roll_rates` count movements between buckets; `roll_rates` requires `states` (best to worst) unless both inputs are ordered categoricals, because a sorted default would mis-order labels such as `current`.

  - **`conditional_pd(probability, *, correlation, factor=None | level=None)`** is Φ((Φ⁻¹(PD) − √ρ z)/√(1 − ρ)) with exactly one of `factor` (z, finite) or `level` (z = −Φ⁻¹(level)); `systematic_factor(default_rate, probability, *, correlation)` inverts it (Series name `factor`). `vasicek_fit(default_rate)` returns `(probability, correlation)`: the mean, and the ρ that solves Φ₂(Φ⁻¹(m), Φ⁻¹(m); ρ) − m² = s² (ddof 1) by bisection to adjacent floats; a constant series gives 0, a variance ≥ m(1 − m) raises.
  - **`most_prudent_pd(event, total, *, level=0.95, correlation=0.0)`** pools each grade with all riskier grades (grades ordered from least to most risky) and returns the largest PD with P(D ≤ d) ≥ 1 − level: the beta quantile of (d + 1, n − d) when independent, else bisection on Φ⁻¹(PD) of the binomial CDF integrated over the factor with composite Gauss–Legendre (64 panels × 8 nodes on [−9, 9]; Gauss–Hermite missed by 1e-4), summed node by node, not by matrix product. A grade whose pooled obligors all defaulted, or that pools none, gets 1. It reproduces the nine tables of Pluto and Tasche (arXiv:cond-mat/0411699v2); four printed cells differ in the last digit and are listed in the test.
- **`kivi.lifetime`**: PD term structures and IFRS 9.
  - `convert_pd(probability, *, kind, to)` converts between cumulative, marginal and conditional PDs; the conversions are exact inverses and keep NaN months. `transition_pd(matrix, *, horizons, default_state=None)` gives the cumulative PD per starting state by powers of a transition matrix whose default row is made absorbing.
  - `survival_table(duration, event, *, competing=None, entry=None, weights=None)` (1.5) has one row per period 1..max(duration) (index `period`): `at_risk`, `event`, `competing` (only with `competing`), `censored`, `hazard`, `survival`, `cumulative_pd`. An account is at risk while `entry < t <= duration`, and the censored accounts of a period are at risk in it. Without `competing`, `cumulative_pd` is 1 − Kaplan–Meier; with it, the Aalen–Johansen cumulative incidence Σ S(s − 1) h_s, with `survival` all-cause and `hazard` cause-specific. A period without accounts at risk has a NaN hazard and NaN `survival`/`cumulative_pd` from then on. `event` and `competing` both 1 raises, as do `entry >= duration`, missing values and booleans in `duration`/`entry`. `at_risk` sums weights over aligned power-of-two blocks (`_at_risk`), so a weight of 1e20 that has left the risk set cannot round away a weight of 1. Checked against statsmodels `SurvfuncRight` (with `entry` and `freq_weights`) and `CumIncidenceRight`, and for complete cohorts against `vintage_table`.
  - `expected_credit_loss(probability, lgd, ead, *, rate, horizon=None)` sums marginal PD × LGD × EAD × (1 + rate)^−t over the periods after the reporting date, with period 1 the first; one row or column broadcasts. Loans already in default are measured at the reporting date (rate 0, PD 1), as the guide shows. The guide's "Economic scenarios" section shifts the conditional PDs of the transition-matrix states with `capital.conditional_pd` (ρ = 0.1, factors 1, 0, −1.5 with weights 0.25/0.5/0.25), keeps PDs of 0 and the 90+ row, and weights the ECL of the scenarios.
  - `significant_increase(probability, origination_probability, *, relative, absolute)` compares strictly, with the thresholds required because they are the lender's policy. `assign_stages(significant_increase, days_past_due=None, defaulted=None, *, stage_2_days=30, stage_3_days=90)` gives stage 2 above 30 days (IFRS 9 5.5.11) and stage 3 at 90 days or more (B5.5.37). `stage_table(stage, exposure, allowance, *, weights)` always has rows 1–3. The staging functions reject missing values.
- **`kivi.plotting`**: `woe_chart`, `correlation_chart`, `roc_chart`, `precision_recall_chart`, `gains_chart`, `distribution_chart`, `calibration_chart`, `cutoff_chart`, `psi_chart`, `monitoring_chart`, `vintage_chart`, and from 1.8 `survival_chart`, `transition_chart`, `rule_set_chart`, `profit_chart` and `lgd_chart` draw kivi's tables.
  - `precision_recall_chart(table, *, label, ax)` draws the `precision` of a `roc_table` against its `true_positive_rate` for the rows with a precision (not the `inf` row), as steps (`drawstyle="steps-pre"`) from recall 0, so the area under the steps is the average precision (scikit-learn's `average_precision_score`, checked in `test_reference.py`), with a gray dashed line at the last row's precision, the event rate, drawn once per rate on an Axes. `distribution_chart(table, *, ax)` draws each row's share of all non-events and of all events (raw counts, not the smoothed `*_share` columns) of any table with `non_event` and `event`, empty rows kept, with the legend in a reserved top row.
  - Each takes the table first and a keyword-only `ax`, draws on a new figure without `ax`, and returns the Axes. `woe_chart`, the one chart with two y-axes, returns the pair (bars, line), as the user decided; `rule_set_chart` draws its bars and its line on one axis and returns one Axes.
  - The 1.8 charts: `survival_chart(table, *, column="cumulative_pd" | "survival", label, ax)` draws steps from the period before the first row (limits from `ax.dataLim`, so tables share an `ax`), and with a `competing` column also the competing cause's cumulative incidence, computed from `survival`, `competing` and `at_risk`; `transition_chart(table, *, ax)` takes a `transition_matrix` (counts or shares, not the Series of `roll_rates`, and at least two columns), shades each cell by its row share, writes compact values (`300M`) and outlines the cells whose row and column labels are equal; `rule_set_chart` draws the `new_hits` of each rule as a share of the first rule's `approved + new_hits` with the `approval_rate` line; `profit_chart(table, *, label, ax)` draws `profit` against `approval_rate` of a `cutoff_table` with `loss` and `revenue` and marks the first maximum (cut-off to four significant digits); `lgd_chart` draws `mean`, `zero_share` and `full_share` per group of an `lgd_table`.
  - The columns are checked before a figure is created. No function calls `show`, changes `rcParams` or mutates its input, and chart text is only column names, argument names and values.
  - matplotlib (extra `plot`, >= 3.7) is imported inside the functions, so `import kivi.plotting` loads no matplotlib; `tests/test_plotting.py` checks that in a subprocess and is skipped without matplotlib.
  - `monitoring_chart(table, *, column, kind, label, levels, ax)` draws bars or a line only by `kind`, never by the column name.
  - `docs/api/compatibility.rst` promises the data drawn, the arguments, the errors and the returned Axes, but not colors, sizes or the placement of legends and annotations.
- **Shared naming:**
  - The data argument comes first and the target second, as in `woe_table(groups, target)`, `roc_auc(score, target)` and `stepwise(X, target)`.
  - A predicted probability argument is always called `probability`.

From 1.0 kivi follows semantic versioning, as `docs/api/compatibility.rst` promises: a deprecation warns with `FutureWarning` for the rest of the major version and takes effect in the next major release, and a fix of a wrong result may land in any release. 1.0 removed what 0.9 deprecated, as the pre-1.0 rule required, and `docs/release/migration.rst` lists those changes. Python 3.10, NumPy 1.24 and pandas 2.0 stay the minimums until 1.9, which requires Python 3.11, as the `python-3-11.changed.rst` fragment of 1.8 announces; from 1.0 a minimum rises only in a minor release that a `changed` fragment of the release before announces, together with `pyproject.toml`, the `minimum-versions` job and the versions on `install.rst` and `compatibility.rst` (`docs/development/releasing.rst`). Deprecations warn through `_deprecation.warn`, which gives the warning to the first frame outside kivi, so it points at the caller's line however deep the check sits. 1.0 itself has no deprecations; the helper stays for those of later 1.x releases. `tests/test_deprecations.py` tests that it points at the caller's line, also from two calls deep inside the package, and each new deprecation adds its test there (`pytest.warns(FutureWarning, match=)` and the test's own file name) until the major release that removes it. Code and docs must never trigger a deprecation warning themselves, because pytest and the doctests treat warnings as errors.

Public functions accept one-dimensional array_like input through `_validation.as_series`, which leaves Series untouched, and return results shaped like the input (Series, float or array) through `_validation.like_input`. Inputs that describe the same observations (data, target, probability, weights) must have identical indices when two of them are Series, checked through `_validation.shared_index` and `as_weights(..., others=)`; arrays and lists match by position. Helpers that several public modules need live in `_validation` (`as_real`, `known_outcomes`, `shared_index`, `finite_scores`, `binary_labels`, `check_class_weights`, `check_count`, `check_smoothing`, `as_generator`, `as_numbers`, `object_index`, `is_missing`, the column helpers), `_counts` (`ratio`, and `concat_groups`, which joins two samples' groups for PSI without hashing an interval `CategoricalDtype`, which warns on NumPy < 2.2, and matches unordered categories by label), `_special` (`special_values`, `special_positions`) or `_edges` (`fit_edges`, `band_edges`, `observed`, `weighted_quantile`). A public module imports no private helper of another public module. Every module imports modules rather than names (`_validation.as_series`, `typing.Literal`, `numbers.Real`, `abc.Mapping` from `from collections import abc`), so a public module binds only its `__all__`, private names and modules; `tests/test_namespaces.py` checks this. `_validation.check_smoothing(smoothing)` requires a real, finite, positive scalar that is not a boolean and returns it; every public function and class with a `smoothing` argument goes through it, and `tests/test_validation.py` checks that the list is complete. Error messages name the argument and show the value received through `_validation.shown` (a Series or Index stands for its first value, NumPy scalars are shown as Python scalars) or `shown_argument` (a whole argument, arrays as lists), and tests check them with `pytest.raises(..., match=)`. The exception and warning types are public, the wording is not (`docs/api/compatibility.rst`).

Every function is annotated, and `src/kivi/py.typed` ships the hints. Use `npt.ArrayLike` for array_like inputs and `float | pd.Series | npt.NDArray[np.float64]` for results shaped by `like_input`; `_validation.DTypeObj` is the type of a NumPy or pandas dtype. A public function whose result type follows the type of an input keeps that union on its implementation and declares `@typing.overload` stubs in front of it (`...` bodies and defaults, no docstrings): `pd.Series` → `pd.Series` (one overload per argument that can make the result a Series), `float` → `float` where a scalar gives a float, `_validation.ListLike` (sequences, `pd.Categorical`, one-dimensional arrays) → an array or `pd.Categorical`, and last a catch-all that repeats the implementation's annotations, so that arguments typed `npt.ArrayLike` keep the union. An overload declares only a type that the function returns for every argument of its types, and `tests/test_typing.py` checks each case with mypy and at run time. Use `_validation.is_real` / `is_integral` instead of `isinstance(x, Real)` / `isinstance(x, Integral)` on a value annotated `float` or `int`, because mypy does not know that `int` and `float` are registered as `numbers.Real` and would skip the rest of the function as unreachable. Every `# type: ignore` takes an error code and a reason. Do not subscript pandas classes (that raises at runtime on pandas 2.0) or use `from __future__ import annotations`.

All metric functions share `_counts.py`, which defines the counting and smoothing semantics. Change behavior there, not per metric:

- `group_counts` uses `pd.factorize(use_na_sentinel=False)`: missing values form their own group and unused categorical levels are dropped. Categorical groups are factorized with `sort=True` (category order, missing last); other groups with `sort=False` (first-observed order). The result index is named `bin`.
- Counting uses `np.bincount` on `2 * code + side`. With `weights` the counts are sums of weights (`_validation.as_weights` checks and aligns them like `target`), and groups whose weights sum to zero are dropped. Weighted quantiles in `bin_edges` and in the supervised fine bins use `_edges.weighted_quantile`, the inverted CDF that `np.quantile(..., weights=, method="inverted_cdf")` uses on NumPy ≥ 2.0, implemented here because NumPy 1.24 has no `weights`. Integer weights give the same counts as repeating rows, which the tests check; quantile edges agree with repeated rows only where no interpolation is involved, because unweighted quantiles interpolate linearly while weighted ones use the inverted CDF (so unit weights are not identical to no weights). `weighted_quantile` subtracts a relative 1e-10 from the probabilities so that a probability on a CDF step selects that step despite float rounding. `smoothing` is in units of weight; it is not rescaled.
- `shares` replaces zero cells with `smoothing` (default 0.5) and then normalizes each column independently. Count columns in the output stay raw; only the `*_share` columns reflect smoothing.
- `divergence(counts, smoothing)` takes an `(n, 2)` count array and returns the shares, `ln(b / a)` and `(b - a) ln(b / a)` per row. With (non-event, event) columns these are WOE and IV, and with (reference, current) columns the PSI terms. `woe_table`, `psi_table`, the count-based `kivi.woe.woe` / `kivi.woe.iv` / `kivi.stability.psi` and the supervised binning helpers all go through it. The public count functions check that two count Series have identical indices with `_validation.shared_index` and then validate the counts with `_validation.as_count_columns`; `woe` returns a Series named `woe` on that index when a count argument is a Series.

Output columns: `woe_table` → `non_event, event, total, non_event_share, event_share, total_share, event_rate, woe, iv` (only the two class shares are smoothed; `total_share` and `event_rate` use raw counts); `psi_table` → `reference, current, reference_share, current_share, psi`. `woe_table` requires `groups` and `target` to have identical indices and both classes present. The index name, the column names and their order of every returned table are public (`docs/api/compatibility.rst` lists them all): a minor release only appends columns, and a PR that adds a table or a column updates that list.

`kivi.datasets.load_bank()` loads the bundled `;`-separated `bank.csv` (UCI Bank Marketing, CC BY 4.0, see `DATA-LICENSE.txt`) via `importlib.resources` and maps `y` yes/no to `target` 1/0. Tests pin its shape (4521×17) and class counts. `make_loan_panel(*, n_loans, n_months, prepayment=0.0, rng)` simulates a long-format instalment loan panel from a local `np.random.default_rng(rng)`; the status is the months since the oldest unpaid instalment fell due (capped at 90+, which is absorbing), so unpaid loans keep rolling past maturity. With `prepayment` (1.5), a loan current at the end of the month before that has instalments left beyond the one due repays its whole balance with that monthly probability: balance 0 before the term and no rows after. The monthly draws are one `(n_months, n_loans)` block and the prepayment draws a second block after it, so the default output is bit-identical to 1.4.0 (SHA-256 digests in `tests/test_datasets.py`) and, with prepayment, every loan follows its path without prepayment until it repays. `make_applications(*, n_applicants, include_risk, rng)` simulates applications decided by an incumbent scorecard that sees part of a hidden risk factor, with policy declines and a true `target` for everyone, so rejects default more often than accepts with the same features; `include_risk=True` appends the hidden risk factor as the last column `risk` (the default output is unchanged). `make_defaults(*, n_defaults=2000, rng)` (1.6) simulates defaulted loans (`loan_id`, `segment` secured/unsecured/revolving, `default_date` 2018–2024, `ead`, `limit` and `drawn` of the revolving lines 12 months before default, `collateral_value`, `discount_rate` = a stylized 3M rate of the default month + 5pp, `closing_date` missing for workouts open at the 30 June 2025 cut-off), and `make_recoveries(defaults, *, rng)` their cash flows (`loan_id`, `date`, `amount`, `kind` recovery/cost/cure): bimodal LGDs, cures as a recovery of the outstanding amount on the closing date, some pre-default legal costs, negative LGDs and LGDs above 1, no flow after closing or the cut-off. `make_transactions(applications, *, application_date, n_months=12, rng)` simulates account transactions for the applicants of `make_applications` from a local generator and returns only the transactions (`applicant_id`, `timestamp`, `amount`, `kind`, `merchant_category`, sorted by applicant and time): purchases, cash withdrawals and instalment repayments in `[date - n_months, date)` (counted as `pandas.DateOffset`), driven by `risk` (not `target`), `income`, `debt_to_income` and `incumbent_pd`, with a bounded stress that builds up before the application, so window features carry a clear but imperfect signal about `target` and remain informative within bands of `incumbent_pd`. `application_date` is required (a column name, one date or a Series matched by index; periods and numbers raise), and a missing `risk` column names `include_risk`.

## Releasing

`.github/workflows/release.yml` runs when a `v*` tag is pushed. The build job fails before anything is uploaded unless the tag is `v` plus the exact `version` in `pyproject.toml` and the tagged commit is on master; it also runs pytest and `twine check --strict` (which renders `README.md`). The publish job uploads to PyPI with trusted publishing through the `pypi` environment (no stored token), and only after that succeeds does a third job create the GitHub release with `gh release create --verify-tag --generate-notes` and the dist files attached (`a`/`b`/`rc`/`dev` versions become pre-releases). Do not create the GitHub release by hand first: the job would fail on the existing release.

Protection lives in repository settings, not in the code: the `pypi` environment only accepts deployments from `v*` tags and has no required reviewers, so a pushed tag is uploaded without further approval; a tag ruleset ("Release tags") lets only admins create, move or delete `v*` tags; PyPI's trusted publisher is bound to `gchensy/kivi` + `release.yml` + environment `pypi`, so renaming the workflow file or environment breaks publishing until PyPI is updated.

Choose the version by `docs/development/releasing.rst`: a patch release only fixes, a minor release adds, deprecates or raises a minimum announced one release earlier, and a major release removes what was deprecated. To release: bump `version`, run `python docs/build.py --release <version>` (it refuses while entry-point gaps remain; otherwise it writes the dated section from the fragments and deletes them) and translate the new heading, merge to master, then `git tag v<version>` on the merged commit, and `git push origin v<version>`. The push publishes to PyPI; there is no approval step to stop it.

## Development workflow

Several Claude sessions often work on this repository at once. These rules keep their pull requests mergeable:

- **Scope first.** The session that splits up the work gives every parallel session a brief naming the files it owns. Two in-flight pull requests must not edit the same file; work that would is done one after the other, and a session that finds it needs a file another open PR changes says so instead of editing it. Usual hotspots: `src/kivi/binning.py`, `src/kivi/_counts.py`, `docs/conf.py`, `docs/user_guide/binning.rst`, `CLAUDE.md`.
- **A feature session only adds its own files**: `src/kivi/<module>.py`, `tests/test_<module>.py`, `docs/user_guide/<topic>.rst`, `docs/api/<module>.rst` and a release-note fragment. The globs pick up the new pages. `kivi.po` is the one shared file every PR may change; resolve its conflicts with `--merge-po`. Feature PRs must not add dependencies; a new runtime dependency or optional extra is the user's decision.
- **Shared entry points change only in an integration PR** that the coordinating session opens after a wave of feature PRs has merged: the README tables, gallery and roadmap (both languages), the title, steps and cards of `docs/index.rst`, the cards and order of `docs/user_guide/index.rst`, `docs/user_guide/risk_workflow.rst`, `docs/release/migration.rst`, the charts the feature PRs describe and this file. It rewrites them for the library as it now is instead of appending (see Entry points in `documentation.rst`).
- **Branch from the latest `origin/master`** (fetch first), one topic per pull request. Stack a pull request on an unmerged branch only when the work depends on it, and say so in its description.
- **Design documents** go in `docs/design/` as Markdown, such as `docs/design/kivi-1.3-features.md` with the user's decisions for 1.3, and for 1.4 `kivi-1.4-legacy-gaps.md` (the 0.2.x tools ported in 1.4) and `kivi-1.4-risk-parameters.md` (the validation tests of 1.4 and the approved plan for 1.5 capital and survival curves, 1.6 LGD/EAD and 1.7 rules and revenue), for 1.5 `kivi-1.5-capital-survival.md` (the check of the Basel, China and ECB texts and the seven decisions behind `kivi.capital` and `survival_table`), for 1.6 `kivi-1.6-lgd-ead.md` (the LGD and CCF texts and the eight decisions behind `kivi.lgd` and the simulated recoveries), for 1.7 `kivi-1.7-rules-profit.md` (the check of the EMP paper, the R package EMP and empulse, and the six decisions behind `kivi.rules`, the revenue of `cutoff_table` and `expected_maximum_profit`), and for 1.8 `kivi-1.8-roadmap.md` (the plan of 1.8 and 1.9, the binning experiments behind `optimal_edges` and the guide's development IV, the five charts, the rule search and the ten decisions) and `kivi-1.8-default-rates.md` (the check of the EBA guidelines, CRE36, the ECB instructions and Tasche's papers, and the nine decisions behind `default_rate_table`, `central_tendency_shift` and `quasi_moment_fit`). Sphinx reads only `.rst`, so they are not part of the built documentation and need no translation; the guides and the migration guide restate what users need.
- **Release notes go in fragments**: add `docs/release/unreleased/<topic>.<category>.rst` (category `new`, `changed`, `fixed` or `removed`) with one bullet item per user-facing change. Never edit the Unreleased section of `docs/release/index.rst`; the `release-fragments` directive in `docs/_fragments.py` renders the fragments there. A fragment is translated like any other text.
- **master is protected** by the repository ruleset "master": no direct or force pushes and no deletion; a change lands through a pull request whose `build` check passed on a branch that is up to date with master, so every PR is tested on top of everything merged before it. Merged branches are deleted automatically.
- **Merging is done on request.** A session merges only pull requests the user asked it to merge, in dependency order: bring the branch up to date by merging `origin/master` into it (not rebasing; PRs with Auto-fix must not be force-pushed), resolve a `kivi.po` conflict with `--merge-po`, wait for `build`, merge with `gh pr merge --merge`, then tell the sessions whose open PRs touch the same files to merge master.
- **Decisions that stay with the user**: priorities and scope, changes to the public API, merges that were not delegated, and releases (pushing a `v*` tag, which publishes to PyPI at once and cannot be undone).

## Scope and design constraints

From `docs/development/legacy_review.rst` and `docs/release/migration.rst`:

- At runtime the core depends only on NumPy and pandas. `kivi.plotting` needs matplotlib through the optional `plot` extra and imports it inside its functions, and scikit-learn, SciPy and statsmodels are references of the tests only (`test` extra). The user allowed scikit-learn as a dependency on 2026-11-22 and approved this split; a new runtime dependency or extra is the user's decision. Infrastructure-specific code (Spark, SQL, etc.) belongs in optional integrations and must not be imported at module load.
- Functions must not mutate caller inputs; anything random takes a local seed instead of touching NumPy's global state.
- **Out of scope.** Spark, SQL and model wrappers exist only in git history before commit 740ea6c. The user decided not to build the Spark and SQL integrations. The features that the Spark tools `FeatureTools`, `FeatureToolsV2` and `index/collect.py` computed are re-implemented in pandas by `kivi.features` (1.3), without their Hive writers; their cross features and industry percentile ranks are not ported, and automatic feature generation and in-population ranks are outside 1.3 (`docs/design/kivi-1.3-features.md`).
- **Re-implemented, not ported.** Sample weights, supervised binning, variable selection, scorecards, metrics, calibration (including Platt and isotonic, against statsmodels and scikit-learn), CSI (against the legacy `evaluate/csi.py`), vintage tables (against the legacy `VintagePd`), batch binning specifications, Best-KS binning (against a re-run of the legacy `KSBins` cut logic, whose IV it matches or beats on the bank columns where `KSBins` does not crash, and against SciPy's `ks_2samp`), the AHP weights and consistency (against the legacy `ahp.py`: the geometric weights agree to 1e-16, and its λ 7.4078 and CR 0.2271 on Saaty's school matrix and CR 0.0433 on a 4×4 example are reproduced by its estimate, while the exact eigenvalue is 7.4199), delinquency panels (against a month-by-month brute force on random books in `tests/test_vintage.py`, and the legacy `Vintage`/`VintagePd` rules, whose differences `migration.rst` lists), time-window and panel features (against brute-force references per observation and pandas rolling windows in `tests/test_features.py`, and the window rules of the legacy feature tools, whose differences `migration.rst` lists), `vif_filter` and `selection_path` (against throwaway copies of the legacy `VIF.LowVIFFeatures` and `StepWise` on the bank WOE data: the largest p-values of the nested models agree to 1.6e-12 and the AUCs to 2.7e-7, where statsmodels' matrix product splits a tie, while the drops differ where the legacy VIF without an intercept or its smallest-sum rule decide), `woe_trend` (one to one with the legacy `monotony` labels on 20,000 random sequences without ties), `expected_maximum_profit` (against values computed with empulse 0.14.0 and a brute-force integration) and `optimal_edges` (against an exhaustive search and values computed with optbinning 1.1.0) were each checked against the legacy outputs or reference implementations (statsmodels, scipy, scikit-learn, mpmath, empulse, optbinning). When porting anything else, validate against fixed reference samples first.
- **`tests/test_reference.py` repeats those cross-checks in CI.** It covers `tree_edges`, the first cut of `ks_edges` (the gap against SciPy's `ks_2samp` statistic always, the location against `statistic_location` only on bank columns without a tied largest gap, and never on `previous`), the isotonic and Platt calibrations, `logistic_regression`, `vif`, `selection_path` (statsmodels GLM and scikit-learn AUC), the ROC metrics and the average precision of the precision-recall steps, `ks`, ChiMerge, `chimerge_groups`, `fit_grade_pd`, the DeLong variance, `kivi._stats` (`student_t_sf` to 1e-12 for 1.5 to 1e9 degrees of freedom; SciPy's `t.sf` with one degree of freedom loses digits near 0, so `test_calibration.py` checks that case against the Cauchy closed form), the calibration tests, the t tests of `backtest_table`, the normal tails of `spiegelhalter_test`, `concentration_test` and `migration_test`, Somers' D of `generalized_auc`, the beta quantile, the bivariate normal and the array normal quantile of `_stats`, the risk-weight functions (an independent SciPy implementation), the moment equation of `vasicek_fit`, `most_prudent_pd` (Clopper–Pearson and `quad`), the Kaplan–Meier and Aalen–Johansen curves of `survival_table` (statsmodels), `fractional_regression` (statsmodels `GLM` with HC0), the two-bin partitions of `variance_edges` (scikit-learn `DecisionTreeRegressor` stumps), `central_tendency_shift` (SciPy `brentq`) and `quasi_moment_fit` (SciPy `root` on the mean PD and scikit-learn's AUC of the implied sample), against scikit-learn, SciPy and statsmodels. Values of libraries that are no test dependency are hard-coded in the module's own test file instead: empulse in `test_metrics.py` and optbinning in `test_supervised_binning.py`. Feature PRs put their cross-checks behind `importorskip` in their own test file, and the integration PR moves them here.
  - Every test does `pytest.importorskip` for its library.
  - Selections chosen from the data are compared exactly, and computed floats with a `rel` tolerance and `abs=0`.
  - A failing cross-check is a possible wrong result: do not loosen it. Mark a documented difference of definition `xfail(strict=True)`.
  - The lower bounds of the `test` extra (scikit-learn 1.3, SciPy 1.12, statsmodels 0.14) were checked locally; CI installs newer releases.
  - Known precision limits of `_stats`: `betainc` at extreme arguments is about 1e-9 relative, and `gammaincc` reaches about 4e-10 relative at a = 1e5. `student_t_sf` stays within about 4e-13 of SciPy for t from 1e-8 to 40 and 1.5 to 1e9 degrees of freedom, thanks to `_t_tail_expansion`. `beta_quantile` solves SciPy's `betainc` to about 1e-12 relative for shapes up to 1e4 (1e-10 up to 1e6), where SciPy's own `beta.ppf` misses some roots, and `bivariate_normal_cdf` keeps about 1e-14 relative for ρ ≥ 0 and probabilities above 1e-10. `docs/release/migration.rst` lists how each one differs from its legacy counterpart.
- Tests assert raw counts, normalized shares and hand-calculated metric values (the legacy tests only printed tables) — follow that pattern.
